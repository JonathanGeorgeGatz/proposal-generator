The Proposal Generator — the exact prompts

Word for word, as sent in the episode. Paste them into your AI assistant in order — prompts 2 and 3 exist because the first two versions were wrong in ways worth understanding before a client signs anything. The tool itself: try it here · download everything (zip)

Prompt 1 — build the whole machine

Build me a single HTML file I can open in my browser, no internet, no account, that turns my
price list into a client-ready proposal.

What it's for: I run a small design-build landscaping company. I already keep the job's line
items in a spreadsheet, and I keep a file of the wording I use — my warranty, my payment terms,
my exclusions. Writing the actual proposal document is the slow part.

Data in: two pastes.
1) The job's line items: CSV with columns Line, Section, Item, Qty, Unit, Unit Price, Optional —
   Optional marks upgrades the client can say yes to.
2) My proposal library: CSV with columns Kind, Fieldname, Text — my business name, the client,
   the project, the date, and the clauses I actually use.

What I want out: the finished proposal — scope grouped by section with prices, the options, a
payment schedule, my terms, and a signature block. Something I could send today.

Prompt 2 — ban invention, split the options

The first version wrote its own document. It promised a comprehensive five-year workmanship
warranty — my library says two years — and "fully licensed, bonded and insured", words I never
gave it. It also priced the job at every row it could read, folded into one total: both options
the client hasn't accepted, a duplicate row, everything. And the rows it couldn't read just
vanished. Rebuild the rules:

- NOT ONE SENTENCE in the document comes from you. Every paragraph is a clause from my library,
  with my field names filled in. If it isn't in the library, it isn't in the proposal.
- Optional lines NEVER join the price. They sit in their own section the client can accept,
  and the contract total only moves when one is accepted.
- The payment schedule is computed from the contract total with the percentages shown.
- Scope is grouped by my sections, each with a subtotal, and the subtotals must add to the base
  price exactly.

Prompt 3 — make it trustworthy enough to sign

Now make it trustworthy enough to sign.

- All money arithmetic in whole integer cents with one written-down rounding rule (round half
  up). The LAST payment in the schedule is the remainder after the others, so the three payments
  always add to the contract total exactly — a schedule that disagrees with its own total by
  even a cent has no place in a signing document.
- Refuse what you can't read, out loud: a quantity that isn't a number, a price that says "TBD",
  a duplicate line id, a blank section. The Optional column accepts yes, no, or blank — anything
  else is refused with its row and the reason, never guessed.
- The valid-until date is computed from the proposal date in my library plus a validity window I
  set. Never from "today" — a document that changes meaning depending on the day you open it is
  not a document.
- Audit the document itself on every run: every curly-brace field filled; every paragraph traced
  back to my library byte for byte, so nothing invented can hide in the terms; lines in = base +
  options + refused; section subtotals re-added against the base price; the schedule re-added
  against the contract total. If any audit fails, or too many rows are refused, print DO NOT
  TRUST THIS PROPOSAL across the top, say why, and withhold the document.
